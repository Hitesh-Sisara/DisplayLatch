Disconnect a display on Mac without unplugging it

Sometimes you want one monitor off: for a call, for focus, to save power, or so your windows don't land on a screen you aren't using. macOS has no built-in switch for this. Here's how to do it for free.

With DisplayLatch (menu bar)

  1. Install DisplayLatch (free and open source).
  2. Click the display icon in the menu bar.
  3. Turn off the switch next to the monitor. It leaves the desktop, and its windows move to your other displays.
  4. Turn the switch back on to restore it in the same place in your arrangement.

From Terminal

displaylatch list
displaylatch disconnect ABC1234567     # by serial, ID or name
displaylatch connect ABC1234567

What "disconnect" means here

The monitor stays physically connected and powered, but macOS stops using it as a screen. That's different from dimming it to black: the space disappears from your arrangement, so the mouse and new windows can't go there. DisplayLatch remembers monitors you disconnected, so its auto-reconnect leaves them off until you turn them back on.

Alternatives

BetterDisplay also has this feature, in its paid Pro version. displayplacer can turn displays off from the command line. Turning off the monitor with its own power button usually works too, but some monitors keep the Mac thinking they're connected.

Frequently asked questions

Can I turn off an external monitor on Mac without unplugging it?

Yes. DisplayLatch adds a switch for each monitor in the menu bar, and the command 'displaylatch disconnect'. Either one takes the display off the desktop without touching the cable.

Will my windows come back when I reconnect the display?

macOS moves windows to the remaining screens when a display is disconnected. When you reconnect it, DisplayLatch restores the display's position in your arrangement. macOS then decides where windows go.
